## Looks at

Who reads the document, what they already know, what they must decide or do after reading, and how much time they will spend.

## Key questions

- Who is the primary reader? Anyone else who will see it?
- What should the reader do or decide after reading?
- What does the reader already know, and which terms need no explanation?
- How will it be read — skimmed on a phone, printed, presented, forwarded?
- What tone and length does this reader expect?

## Common gaps

- The document lists what was done but not what the reader should do with it.
- Detail level fits the writer, not the reader (too much for an executive, too little for a reviewer).
- Format does not match the channel (a long document for a chat message).

## Turning gaps into questions

Infer reader and channel from the request first ("팀장에게" → manager, short, decision-oriented). Ask only when the reader's required action is unclear and changes the content.
Example: "이 보고로 팀장님이 해 주셨으면 하는 게 있나요? ① 없음, 공유만(추천 — 주간 보고는 보통 공유용) ② 결정 요청(일정·인력) ③ 도움 요청 — 요청이 있으면 맨 위에 올려요."
Otherwise assume and label it.

## Tensions

- With **document skeletons**: a standard skeleton wants every section; the reader may need only two — ask which sections the reader actually reads.
- With **SCQA**: SCQA puts the answer first; some readers (e.g. a formal approval line) expect background first — keep the answer in the first paragraph and the order the channel requires.

## When not to use

The output is not read by a person (a prompt for another AI uses the task brief instead), or the reader is the requester only.

## Output form

Form: a reader card — 독자 · 읽은 뒤 할 일 · 아는 것 · 읽는 방식 · 어조와 길이.
Link to first layer: each answered question fills one field; the "할 일" becomes the first line of the document.
When to produce: kept internal for L0 (shown only as assumptions); written out for proposals and decks (L1+).

## Closing

End with the one action the reader should take and the length/tone chosen for it — not a description of the reader.
