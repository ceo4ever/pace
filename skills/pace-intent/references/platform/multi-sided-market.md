## Looks at

The sides the platform connects, the core interaction between them, who pays and who is subsidised, and how trust and quality are kept.

## Key questions

- What are the sides, and what is the one core interaction (a match, a booking, a delivered job)?
- Which side is harder to attract, and which side pays?
- How is quality controlled — reviews, verification, escrow, disputes?
- What stops both sides from leaving the platform after the first match (disintermediation)?

## Common gaps

- Only one side's screens are planned.
- No answer to who pays.
- Disputes, refunds, and payouts missing (money between users is the platform's liability).

## Turning gaps into questions

Map sides and the core interaction from the request; ask the pricing-side decision and the trust mechanism one at a time.
Example: "편집자와 크리에이터가 플랫폼 밖에서 바로 거래하는 걸 어떻게 막을까요? ① 결제를 플랫폼 안 에스크로로만(추천 — 분쟁 처리와 수수료가 함께 묶여요) ② 연락처 비공개만 ③ 막지 않고 구독으로 수익 · 직접 입력 — 결제·정산 범위와 법적 책임이 달라져요."
Escrow and payouts are regulated in many countries — research the rules (source, date) before assuming.

## Tensions

- With **unit economics**: subsidising the hard side lowers margin — ask how long the subsidy lasts.
- With **MoSCoW**: trust features (reviews, escrow) compete with growth features — ask which trust feature is a Must for the first interaction.

## When not to use

Single-sided products, even with many users; internal tools.

## Output form

Form: a side map — 측 · 얻는 것 · 내는 것(돈/시간/콘텐츠) · 핵심 상호작용 · 신뢰 장치 · 이탈 방지.
Link to first layer: JTBD per side; lean canvas value per side; pre-mortem adds disputes and fraud.
When to produce: L3 platform requests; it decides how the work splits into backlogs (one per side or per interaction).

## Closing

End with the core interaction, the paying side, and the first trust mechanism — and the backlog split they imply.
